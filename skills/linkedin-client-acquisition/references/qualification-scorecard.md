# Evidence-Based LinkedIn Qualification Scorecard

Use this scorecard for service buyers, not recruiters and not generic lead-volume campaigns. The score is a transparent prioritization aid. It is not a measured probability of purchase and must never be presented as one.

## Six dimensions

| Dimension | Maximum | What earns points | What does not earn points |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Need | 300 | Explicitly requesting a developer/agency; publicly describing a website, ecommerce, dashboard, CRM, integration, or automation problem; a verified project requirement | Owning a business; generic founder status; a vague assumption that every company needs a website |
| Service fit | 250 | The observed problem maps directly to a service the user offers, with enough detail to identify a relevant capability | Broad industry similarity without a technical need |
| Authority | 150 | Founder, owner, CEO, agency owner, CTO, technical lead, or a named project owner with evidence of decision influence | A title that sounds senior but has no evidence of decision authority |
| Timing | 150 | A recent request, launch, expansion, new project, relevant hiring need, or current website issue verified within 30 days | Old posts, stale announcements, an undated assumption, or an ordinary job change |
| LinkedIn reachability | 100 | First-degree connection, active public LinkedIn conversation, visibly available Open Profile route, or a current and usable connection path | Assuming that a profile can receive a DM merely because it exists |
| Business capacity | 50 | Evidence the business is operating and resourced enough for the specific project: active store, real product, team, current launch, or explicit project scope | Guessing revenue, budget, company size, or willingness to pay |

## Score interpretation

- **900–1000:** Exceptional fit and unusually clear buying evidence. Still verify every fact.
- **800–899:** Qualified for the daily outreach queue when all evidence gates pass.
- **650–799:** Promising research, but not ready for first-touch outreach.
- **400–649:** Weak or incomplete fit; retain only if a concrete missing fact can be checked.
- **0–399:** Reject or archive.

The threshold is not a substitute for evidence. A 980 score with no source URL or no dated buying signal is **Needs Review**, not Qualified.

Additional hard gates: signal strength must be explicit or strong; need must score at least 180/300; service fit at least 150/250; reachability at least 25/100; and a real LinkedIn contact path must be verified. Funding, growth, founder status, or ordinary internal hiring alone cannot qualify. A hiring signal requires explicit evidence that the company is seeking external contractors, an agency, or implementation support.

## Mandatory evidence gate

Every qualified record must contain:

1. A valid LinkedIn person-profile URL (linkedin.com/in/...).
2. At least one real HTTPS source URL supporting the reason to approach.
3. A concise evidence summary describing what the source actually says or shows.
4. A signal type and date.
5. Evidence status verified, not inferred from a title alone.
6. A six-part integer score breakdown within the permitted ranges.
7. No hard disqualifier.

Use Needs Review if any required evidence is missing, stale, contradictory, or inaccessible. Do not silently convert unknown information into zero or infer a budget.

## Buying-signal strength

**Strong**
- A founder explicitly asks for a Shopify/web developer, development partner, redesign, app, dashboard, or integration recommendation.
- A company posts a scoped project request or describes a technical issue that matches the user's services.
- An agency publicly asks for implementation capacity or white-label development support.

**Moderate**
- A new product/store launch has a verifiable technical gap.
- A public company update describes expansion that creates a specific web or systems requirement.
- A current hiring description explicitly identifies work that could be contracted externally. Hiring alone is not proof that the company wants a freelancer.

**Weak / not sufficient**
- A person is a founder, CEO, owner, CTO, or agency operator.
- A company has a website that looks old without a verified issue or clear reason to approach.
- A profile is active, has many followers, or posts frequently.
- A funding announcement with no connection to the services offered.
- A generic growing-company statement.

## Hard disqualifiers

Reject when the candidate is a competitor, unrelated to the service offer, not a decision-maker and has no route to the buyer, a current customer already in the CRM, already marked not interested, a duplicate, or a profile with unverifiable identity. Respect explicit requests not to be contacted.

## Worked examples

### Example A: explicit Shopify request

A founder publicly asks for recommendations for a Shopify developer for an upcoming store launch. The post is accessible, dated within the last week, and the profile matches the company. This can qualify if service fit, authority, reachability, and capacity are also supported.

### Example B: impressive founder profile

A founder has a large following and operates a successful company, but no current need, request, launch, technical gap, or relevant signal is found. This is not a qualified prospect. Do not manufacture a personalized message from their biography.

### Example C: old redesign post

A company posted about redesigning its website eight months ago. The page still looks dated, but no current project need is verified. The old post is stale. Search for a new signal or mark Needs Review; do not score it as current intent.

## Scoring discipline

Write one sentence of evidence for every non-zero score component. If the source does not support the points, reduce the score or mark the component unknown. Do not award points simply to cross 800. The score should explain why this person is a better use of the user's limited outreach time than the next candidate.
